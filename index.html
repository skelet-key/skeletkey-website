Battery Technology

High-density NMC power. Slide-in modular design.

The energy core is a high-energy-density 16S2P NMC pouch-cell pack engineered for maximum range at minimal weight — and designed to slide cleanly in and out of the frame in seconds.

Modular NMC pack seated between the thin aluminum rails of the rear exoskeleton cradle

16S2P NMC High-Density Chemistry

Large-format NMC pouch cells arranged in a 16-series, 2-parallel configuration deliver a nominal 60.8V pack with approximately 8.5 kWh of usable energy. NMC chemistry provides one of the highest energy densities available in production electric motorcycles, enabling the 100+ mile range target while keeping the complete module at roughly 55–60 lb.

Isolated Exoskeleton Cradle

To protect the central folding hinge, the battery never sits inside the frame cavity. Instead it rides in an isolated structural exoskeleton cradle clamped directly to the non-folding rear main triangle — positioned between the seat and the rear carriage.

Slide-In Locking Mechanism

Two very thin 6061-T6 aluminum guide rails form the sides of the cradle. The pack slides longitudinally along these rails until the positive and negative power contacts mate with the fixed Anderson-style quick-connect terminals. Dual over-center stainless-steel cam latches then lock the pack firmly in place, creating both the mechanical retention and the low-impedance electrical connection in one motion.

Story

From Amsterdam folding bikes to a motorcycle that travels.

The idea for SkeletKey was conceived in Amsterdam, inspired by the city’s culture of folding e-bikes — machines that disappear into apartments, trains, and daily life. The question was simple: why shouldn’t a real electric motorcycle fold the same way?

While building the first prototypes and waiting on my Dutch wife’s K-1 visa to be approved, I traveled with the frame four times back and forth. The bike had to move as easily as we did.

We’re now happily married for over two years and living in New York City. SkeletKey is the machine that came out of that journey — a folding, modular, long-range electric motorcycle built for people who refuse to choose between performance and portability.

History

Iterating until the fold was light enough to matter.

Before the production plan, we built and rebuilt. Each generation of the Puca prototype taught us what to keep — and what to strip away.

Early builds

First complete machines proved the folding concept and long-range battery could coexist — but weight and complexity were still too high for real apartment and travel use.

Battery & function experiments

We tested layouts, pack sizes, and locking systems. Lessons from these iterations drove the shift to ultra-dense semi-solid NMC modules that lock and unlock in seconds.

Stripped to the essentials

We settled on the lightest configuration possible: a low-weight 6061-T6 folding chassis (~79 lb without battery) and modular energy-dense packs designed for apartment living and travel.

Production direction

Those lessons became SkeletKey — focused on foldability, modular power, and the lowest practical chassis weight so the bike fits elevators, trunks, and real city life.
