## 1. Executive System Architecture & Framework

This comprehensive, pageless engineering blueprint consolidates the complete structural, mechanical, and electrical requirements for building the high-performance folding electric motorcycle prototype. The production model is **source the folding frame and parts; contract assemblers to 5,000/year** — not a SkeletKey frame factory. The prototype uses a measured **9.2 lb carbon steel folding frame** to carry a high-energy-density **~63 V / ~8.5 kWh / ~60 lb NMC+ module** and a **QS205 V3 (50H) 3T Spoke Hub Motor** (20×4.0, **13.0 kg / 28.6 lbs**, 4,000–5,000W continuous / ~12,000W peak) with **FarDriver ND72360 BT**. 

By pairing a featherweight structural frame with a massive power system, this architecture is engineered to reach speeds of **up to 70 mph** while providing an extended range of **over 100 miles** under precise cruising management.

## 4. Aerodynamics & 100-Mile Range Optimization

Sustaining a **40 mph cruise speed over 100 miles** requires keeping net energy consumption under **85 Wh/mile**. At 40 mph, aerodynamic drag constitutes roughly 75% of total power loss.

* **Aerodynamic Penalty:** An unfaired fat-tire bicycle profile exhibits a poor drag coefficient (Cd approx 1.15). 
* **Windscreen Modification:** Installing a **15-inch Krator Smoke Windscreen** swept back at a 45° to 55° rake angle lowers the overall area drag coefficient by **18% to 25%**.
* **Range Verification:** Without a windscreen, energy draw spans **95–110 Wh/mile** (limiting range to 77–89 miles). With the windscreen fairing optimized, efficiency yields **78–83 Wh/mile**, securing the 100-mile target from the 8.5 kWh pack. The QS205 V3 (50H) at only 13.0 kg (28.6 lb) further reduces unsprung weight, subtly improving stop-and-go efficiency.

## 6. Controller App Configuration & Thermal Dynamics

### Thermal Joule Heating Loss:
At a continuous current draw of **150A**, internal heat buildup inside the battery pack must be actively dissipated. Given an internal cell resistance of 1.2 mΩ:
* R(pair) = 1.2 mΩ / 2 = 0.6 mΩ
* R(pack) = 0.6 mΩ * 16 = 9.6 mΩ = 0.0096 Ω
* P(heat) = I^2 * R = (150 A)^2 * 0.0096 Ω = **216 Watts**

Without cooling, the system experiences a continuous temperature climb of **0.65°C per minute**. The aluminum enclosure pan must act as a direct heat-sink chassis.

### Safe Programming Configuration Profile:
* **Max Battery Current (DC Amps):** Lock to 150A continuous.
* **Peak Boost Current Limit:** Restrict to 300A max for under 3 seconds.
* **Low-Voltage Cutoff:** 48.0V (3.0V per cell floor threshold to prevent cell inversion).
* **High-Voltage Cutoff:** 67.5V (4.2V per cell ceiling to prevent over-charge plating).
* **Throttle Ramping Up-Time:** Set to 0.3 seconds to smooth aggressive current inrush.
* **Motor Thermal Cutback Threshold:** Enable at 130°C using the integrated KTY83 thermistor inputs.

## 8. Mechanical Fabrication & Rear Dropout Geometry

To securely mesh the motor and frame, your co-founder must execute machining adjustments following these layout rules:

* **Over-Locknut Distance (OLD):** Standardize frame swingarm spacing to **170mm or 190mm** to clear oversized fat rims and prevent chain line interference against tire walls.
* **Axle Seating:** Structural slot entries must measure exactly **10mm wide** to hold the flat shoulders of the M16 axle. Depth must achieve a minimum **16mm engagement depth**.
* **Axle Isolation Principle:** Never allow the steel axle flats to rest directly against bare frame dropout slots. Protect dropout faces under launching torque so axle paths are not damaged. Install the custom 5mm 4130 steel torque arms externally to take 100% of the rotational force.
* **Brake Assembly Clearance:** Weld the rear disc brake caliper bracket to match a strict **15.5mm offset** from the internal left dropout plate surface to align the rotor perfectly through hydraulic pad paths.

## Hub Motor Thermal Protection — Statorade (Grin / Ferrotec)

**Source:** [Grin Tech — Statorade product info](https://ebikes.ca/product-info/grin-products/statorade.html)

Statorade is a low-viscosity magnetically suspended motor cooling fluid jointly developed by **Grin Tech** and **Ferrotec**. It is the primary thermal intervention specified for the **QS205** direct-drive hub on Puca when sustained high power is required.

### Purpose
Cool **outrunner direct-drive hub motors** by transferring heat from the stator copper to the outer motor shell via fluid convection in the magnetic air gap — roughly **doubling average thermal conductivity** from windings to the environment.

### How it works
- Nanomagnetic particles in a stable low-viscosity synthetic oil concentrate in the magnetic air gap.
- Rotor magnets hold the fluid in place so it does not splash or leak under normal RPM.
- Heat moves from the stator to the shell, then to ambient air (pair with aluminum **Hubsinks** for an additional ~15–20% shell-to-air improvement).
- Negligible rolling-drag impact when the correct volume is used.

### Compatibility (critical)
- **Compatible:** Outrunner direct-drive hubs (magnets on the outside) — including **QS205**, Crystalyte, MXUS, Grin All-Axle, 9C+, TDCM-class motors with adequate side-cover sealing.
- **Not effective:** Geared hubs (shell not the heat path) and typical mid-drive inrunners.
- Side covers must seal; Grin motors often ship with fill ports. Non-ported motors need a sealed injection method (port, disk hole, or fill with cover removed then resealed).

### Fill volume (typical direct-drive hub)
- Measurable improvement from **2–3 mL**
- Full effect usually **5–8 mL** at modest RPM; **8–12 mL** at higher RPM
- Add in small increments while watching no-load current — stop when drag rises without thermal benefit
- Excess fluid only adds viscous drag

### Performance claims (Grin testing)
- Example: thermal conductivity from ~1.8 to ~4.3 W/°C with 6 mL in a TDCM test case; core temp from nearly 90°C to under 60°C under comparable load
- Roughly **~40% higher sustained torque/power** before overheating in tested setups
- Faster recovery after high-power bursts; especially helpful at low speed where airflow is weak
- Fluid longevity on the order of **tens of thousands of km** with minimal loss when sealed correctly

### Warnings
- Do **not** substitute cheap eBay ferrofluids — they are not formulated for sustained motor heat and agitation.
- Poor sealing → seepage at speed.
- Overfill → drag without cooling gain.
- Centripetal-force limits depend on motor diameter/RPM; stay within Grin guidance for the hub class.

### Puca application note
For the **QS205 V3 (50H)** spoke hub at 4–5 kW continuous / 12 kW peak on a **~60.8 V nominal** pack, Statorade + optional Hubsinks is the preferred passive cooling path so the motor can hold high phase current without immediate thermal cutback (controller KTY83 threshold remains **130°C**).

## QS205 3T Winding — 70 mph on a ~60 V Nominal Pack

**Question:** Can a QS205 **3T** hit **70 mph** with a **60-volt** (nominal) battery?  
**Answer:** **Yes, under ideal conditions** — the 3T’s higher KV speed profile can reach the target without a 72 V pack upgrade — but the system will be at its limits and must be configured carefully.

### Speed math (3T)
- Approximate **KV ≈ 16.3–17.7 RPM/V** for QS205 3T class windings.
- A **16S** lithium pack is ~**67.2 V** full and ~**59–61 V** nominal under load.
- **Unloaded RPM** at 67.2 V: about **67.2 × 17.7 ≈ 1,189 RPM**.
- **Loaded RPM** (~15% drop for aero/road load): about **~1,010 RPM**.
- On a **26″ bicycle-equivalent** or comparable **~19″ motorcycle** tire circumference, that maps to roughly **74–78 mph** at full charge and about **70 mph** at nominal voltage — **if** voltage and current hold.

### Critical bottlenecks at 70 mph on 60 V
1. **Amperage demand** — Fighting aero load at 70 mph on ~60 V requires high continuous power. Target hardware: battery + controller capable of roughly **150–200 A DC continuous** and **350 A+ phase** class capability (aligned with Puca’s high-phase 150 A controller path; verify phase rating for 3T builds).
2. **Voltage sag** — Weak cells or a low-C BMS will sag (e.g. 60 V → ~52 V) and collapse top speed into the **50–60 mph** band. Use **high-discharge cells** (e.g. Molicel P42A / P45B class or equivalent) and a BMS/pack designed for the continuous current.
3. **Rapid overheating** — High phase current at lower voltage dumps heat into the stator. A 3T held near 70 mph on 60 V will heat faster than a lower-KV wind on 72 V/84 V. **Statorade + Hubsinks** and the **130°C motor thermal cutback** are mandatory, not optional.

### Build rules for a safe 60 V + 3T attempt
- Confirm tire circumference used in the speed math matches the production fat-tire setup.
- Hold bus voltage under load (cells, BMS, wiring, connectors).
- Inject **Statorade** at the recommended volume; monitor no-load current after fill.
- Enable motor temperature cutback via KTY83 (or equivalent) at **130°C**.
- Prefer a controller with verified phase-current headroom for 3T at this power level.
- Validate on a closed course before road use; sustained 70 mph is a thermal and legal edge case.

### Relation to current Puca baseline
Production messaging uses **QS205 V3 (50H)** with **70 mph** top speed on the **60.8 V / ~8.5 kWh** modular pack. If a **3T** wind is selected for a higher-KV speed profile, treat the above as the engineering gate checklist — same pack voltage family, stricter current and cooling discipline.

## Café Racer Seat Mounting (Two-Up / Café Racer Seat Pan)

Here’s a clear technical diagram of how a typical two-seater (two-up) café racer seat mounts to a custom motorcycle frame:

**Key connection points typically used on custom café racers:**

* **Front of the seat pan** – usually bolts or tabs that secure under the rear of the tank or to the upper frame rails.

* **Rear of the seat pan** – bolts through rubber bushings into the rear subframe loops or seat stays (the most common load-bearing mounts).

* **Side brackets (optional)** – additional supports that clamp or bolt to the frame tubes for extra rigidity on longer two-up seats.

These mounts keep the seat solid while allowing a slight amount of isolation from vibration. Exact hardware varies by builder, but the principle (**seat pan → rubber isolators → frame/subframe**) is nearly universal on custom café racers.
